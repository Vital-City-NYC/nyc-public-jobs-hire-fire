How these flowcharts were built

. An explanatory map of law and practice, not legal advice.

What the charts show

For each of five New York City jobs, one chart follows a candidate from the first requirement (an exam or a state license) to permanent status, and a second follows an allegation of misconduct or incompetence to a completed firing. "Fired for cause" excludes layoffs, resignations and retirements. Each chart shows the normal sequence, not every exception a real case could turn on.

Sources, in order of priority

  1. New York State statutes and regulations: Civil Service Law, Education Law, Civil Practice Law and Rules, Public Officers Law, Criminal Procedure Law and Title 8 of the state education regulations.
  2. The New York City Charter, Administrative Code and Rules of the City of New York.
  3. City Department of Citywide Administrative Services personnel rules and the current notice of examination for each title.
  4. Agency documents: hiring pages and handouts, disciplinary guidelines and reports, Chancellor's regulations, and federal court monitor reports where a consent decree governs discipline.
  5. Collective bargaining agreements published by the city Office of Labor Relations or the union.
  6. Court decisions, used only for settled doctrine, such as the rule that probationers can be let go without a hearing absent bad faith.

How clocks are labeled

TagMeaning
StatutoryFixed by state or city law.

Statutory deadlines are limits, not predictions. A disciplinary case that meets every deadline can still take many months, and the stats shown come from each agency's or monitor's own reports.

Verification

  • Each source records the exact page consulted and a short verbatim quote supporting the claim it backs.
  • On Sept. 11, 2026, a script re-fetched every source and checked that each quote appears in the live text. PDFs were converted to text first. Quotes split by ellipses were checked piece by piece. Near-misses caused by PDF line breaks, tables or typographic characters were inspected by hand.
  • The state legislature's site blocks automated requests, so statute quotes were checked against the NewYork.Public.Law mirror while the links point to the official nysenate.gov text.
  • The city code library at codelibrary.amlegal.com only loads in a browser. Section links there were opened in a browser to confirm each went to the right section. Charter quotes were cross-checked against BetaNYC's copy of the Charter.
  • Where an exam notice and an older agency web page disagreed, for example on age or college-credit minimums, the chart follows the notice and the conflict is listed below.
